I · Geography

There is no continent called Europe.

There is Eurasia. And at its western end, a peninsula that drew a line across the land and decided it was a world of its own. The line has moved for twenty-five centuries: the Phasis, the Don, the Urals, the Bosphorus. Nobody has ever agreed where it goes. That is the first clue that Europe was never found. It was invented.

II · The name

Even its name is a rumour.

Herodotus, the first historian, admitted that no one knew who had named it, and noted that Europa, the princess of the myth, came from Asia and never set foot in the land that carries her name. Twenty-five centuries later we still do not know where the word comes from. We only know that, every time it was spoken with enough conviction, something real began to happen.

III · The drafts

Europe has been written in drafts.

Emperors, popes, philosophers, prisoners and diplomats have each tried to write it. Some imposed their draft by force. Others proposed it with words. Force drafts collapsed. Thought drafts keep coming back.

IV · The word

Europeism

/ˌjʊə.rəˈpiː.ɪ.zəm/ · noun

  1. The practice of thinking Europe: proposing, testing and rewriting ideas of what this corner of Eurasia could be together.
  2. Not to be confused with Europeanism. Europeanism asks who Europeans are. Europeism asks what Europe could be.

Origin: from Spanish europeísmo and French européisme. Rare in English. We are borrowing it back.

V · Twelve theses

  1. IEurope is not a place. It is a sentence, rewritten for twenty-five centuries.
  2. IIThere is no European continent. There is a peninsula that decided to be one.
  3. IIIEvery Europe began as a few words that enough people chose to believe.
  4. IVThe Europes imposed by force have always collapsed. The Europes imagined in common keep returning.
  5. VThe European Union is not the end of the idea of Europe. It is its latest draft.
  6. VIA draft that believes it is final stops being written, and starts being defended.
  7. VIITo think Europe is to make it exist. To stop thinking it is to let it dissolve.
  8. VIIIThe question is not who Europeans are. It is what Europe could be.
  9. IXBorders, flags, anthems and treaties are tools of the imagination, not laws of nature.
  10. XEvery generation inherits a Europe. Few dare to write their own.
  11. XIThe next draft will not come from one emperor, one treaty or one capital.
  12. XIIIt will be written together, or it will not be European.
