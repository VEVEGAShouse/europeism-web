Europeism A palimpsest in 19 drafts Draw draft 19

    Europe does not
    exist.

    — unless we keep thinking it.

    Scroll to read it rewritten ↓

    I · Geography

    There is no continent called Europe.

    There is Eurasia. And at its western end, a peninsula that drew a line across the land and decided it was a world of its own. The line has moved for twenty-five centuries: the Phasis, the Don, the Urals, the Bosphorus. Europe was never found. It was invented.

    II · The name

    Even its name is a rumour.

    Herodotus admitted that no one knew who had named it. Europa, the princess of the myth, came from Asia and never set foot in the land that carries her name. Every time the word was spoken with enough conviction, something real began to happen.

    III · The drafts

    Europe has been written in drafts.

    Emperors, popes, philosophers, prisoners and diplomats have each tried to write it. Watch the map. Drafts imposed by force break and fade. Drafts proposed in thought stay on the page.

    IV · The word

    Europeism

    /ˌjʊə.rəˈpiː.ɪ.zəm/ · noun

    1. 1The practice of thinking Europe: proposing, testing and rewriting ideas of what this corner of Eurasia could be together.
    2. 2Not to be confused with Europeanism. Europeanism asks who Europeans are. Europeism asks what Europe could be.

    From Spanish europeísmo and French européisme. Rare in English. We are borrowing it back.

    V · Twelve theses

    1. IEurope is not a place. It is a sentence, rewritten for twenty-five centuries.
    2. IIThere is no European continent. There is a peninsula that decided to be one.
    3. IIIEvery Europe began as a few words that enough people chose to believe.
    4. IVThe Europes imposed by force have always collapsed. The Europes imagined in common keep returning.
    5. VThe European Union is not the end of the idea of Europe. It is its latest draft.
    6. VIA draft that believes it is final stops being written, and starts being defended.
    7. VIITo think Europe is to make it exist. To stop thinking it is to let it dissolve.
    8. VIIIThe question is not who Europeans are. It is what Europe could be.
    9. IXBorders, flags, anthems and treaties are tools of the imagination, not laws of nature.
    10. XEvery generation inherits a Europe. Few dare to write their own.
    11. XIThe next draft will not come from one emperor, one treaty or one capital.
    12. XIIIt will be written together, or it will not be European.
